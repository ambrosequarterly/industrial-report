Key Themes Q3 2026:
• Demand cooled but stayed positive in 10 of 11 markets. Chicago (3.6M SF, +52% YoY), Cincinnati (3.0M SF, third straight 2.5M+ quarter), Nashville (2.7M SF), Columbus (2.5M SF), and Indianapolis (2.1M SF) led; Phoenix slowed to 1.6M SF from 4.6M SF in Q2.
• Vacancy compression continued in Cincinnati (3.0%), Columbus (4.7%, −70 bps QoQ, lowest since 2023), Indianapolis (5.7%, fifth straight quarterly decline), and Miami (6.9%, −40 bps QoQ). Supply-driven upticks hit Nashville (+40 bps), Louisville (+30 bps), and Cleveland (+20 bps).
• Pipelines are rebuilding fast. Phoenix (21.1M SF, +85% YoY), Columbus (14.4M SF, +168% YoY), Chicago (13.2M SF, 54% spec), and Indianapolis (8.9M SF, highest since Q3 2023) are adding supply; Nashville (5.1M SF, −39.6% QoQ) and Louisville (4.0M SF) contracted as projects delivered.
• Rent growth is uneven: Columbus (+14.2% YoY), Orlando (+9.6%), and Miami (+7.4%) led; Denver asking rents fell 2.2% YoY and Cleveland was flat QoQ.
• Soft spots: Cleveland posted −335K SF of absorption on sublease additions; Orlando absorbed just 25K SF; Phoenix's Southwest Valley recorded its first negative quarter in over a decade after UNIS vacated 900K SF.
• Macro (per CBRE Q3 reports): The U.S. economy remains on track to grow by over 2% in 2026, supported by AI-driven business investment and a generally stable labor market. Elevated energy costs and interest rates persist, and the investment side of the market must contend with 5% Treasury yields that CBRE expects to stay elevated for the foreseeable future. A de-escalation in the U.S./Iran conflict is the key upside catalyst, which could ease inflation and give the Fed more flexibility.
• Cap rate data on each market page remains sourced from the CBRE H2 2025 Cap Rate Survey — the most recent edition on file.

Market Overview

Market Overview

Miami's existing stock is the tightest it has been in more than a year. Vacancy fell 40 bps to 6.9% after three quarterly increases — 5.4% excluding buildings delivered since 2025 — as net absorption accelerated for a third straight quarter to 1.27M SF, bringing the year to 2.63M SF. Demand is moving north: Miami Lakes, North Central Dade, and Medley have absorbed 2.76M SF YTD, while Airport/Doral has given back 302K SF. Leasing reached 4.33M SF (+13.5% YoY), with 100K+ SF deals at 40% of volume. The next two quarters will test momentum: ~1.9M SF of signed leases move in during Q4, but 3.4M SF of listed space (incl. 808K SF of sublease) is also coming available.

Market Overview

The pipeline grew 10.4% QoQ to 13.2M SF across 41 properties, and asking rents have risen 23.4% from $6.86 in Q3 2023 to $8.46 (NNN).

Market Overview

Embedded Reports

  • Each market's "View CBRE Report" button opens the CBRE Industrial Figures PDF stored directly in this repository, rather than linking to CBRE's live website
  • PDF filenames follow the pattern [Market]-q3-2026.pdf — capitalized market name plus quarter (e.g. Chicago-q3-2026.pdf); filenames are case-sensitive and must match the button links exactly — and sit alongside index.html
  • Each quarter, add the new PDFs to the repository using the same pattern and update the button paths to the new quarter (e.g. -q4-2026.pdf)
  • All market statistics are taken from the CBRE Q3 2026 Industrial Figures report for that market; cap rate boxes are sourced from the CBRE H2 2025 Cap Rate Survey
